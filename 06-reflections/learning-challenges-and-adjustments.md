# Learning Challenges and Adjustments

This document reflects on some of the challenges I encountered while building my cybersecurity learning path and the adjustments I made along the way.

My learning process has not been completely linear. It has evolved through experimentation, mistakes, reflection, and gradual improvement.

## Finding a Starting Point

One of the first challenges was understanding where to begin.

Cybersecurity includes many different domains, certifications, tools, technologies, and career paths. For someone entering the field from another professional background, it can be difficult to understand which foundations should come first.

I chose the Google Cybersecurity Professional Certificate as my initial structured path because it provided a broad introduction to the field.

At the same time, I started using TryHackMe to introduce practical learning alongside theory.

## Balancing Theory and Practice

During the first months, I divided my study time mostly between structured coursework and hands-on practice.

At one point, I reduced practical learning because I felt that combining both approaches was slowing my progress through the main course.

Later, I realized that removing too much hands-on practice made the learning process less effective for me.

As practical exercises became more important, I reintroduced TryHackMe and started exploring networking concepts through tools such as Wireshark in my own authorized environment.

This helped turn abstract concepts into something observable and practical.

## Going Too Deep Too Early

Another challenge was trying to understand every topic in too much depth immediately.

I created extensive handwritten notes, repeated reviews, and large amounts of personal study material.

Although this helped reinforce some concepts, it also consumed significant time.

Over time, I learned that not every topic needs the same level of depth during the first encounter.

Some concepts are better understood at a foundational level first and explored more deeply later.

This changed the way I prioritize my studies.

## Recognizing the Importance of Networking

As my cybersecurity studies progressed, I increasingly recognized the importance of networking, infrastructure, operating systems, and hardware fundamentals.

Looking back, stronger networking foundations earlier in my learning path would have made several cybersecurity concepts easier to understand.

Rather than treating this as a reason to restart my learning path, I decided to complete the foundation I had already built and progressively strengthen my networking knowledge.

## Studying in a Second Language

Portuguese is my native language, while most of my cybersecurity education is conducted in English.

This adds another layer to the learning process.

Studying a technical subject often requires me to understand both the concept and the terminology used to describe it in English.

Translation, repetition, technical vocabulary, and professional English therefore became part of the same learning process.

I also noticed that revisiting cybersecurity concepts in Portuguese sometimes helped me understand ideas from another perspective.

## Balancing Work and Study

Another important challenge has been balancing cybersecurity learning with full-time night work and normal personal responsibilities.

In the beginning, I sometimes tried to compensate for limited time by studying for long periods and reducing rest.

Over time, I realized that more hours of study do not automatically produce better learning.

Planning, consistency, and recovery became increasingly important parts of my methodology.

## Learning the Importance of Rest

One of the most important adjustments was understanding the role of rest.

Earlier in my transition, I placed significant pressure on myself to study continuously.

This sometimes resulted in long study sessions, limited sleep, and cognitive fatigue.

I gradually noticed that after sufficient rest, my concentration, comprehension, and ability to retain technical concepts improved considerably.

Because of this, recovery is now part of the learning system rather than something separate from it.

Days with less screen time, lighter content, or complete breaks can help make the following study sessions more productive.

## Adapting the Learning Format

I also learned that not every study session needs to involve dense technical material.

When concentration is lower, I may use lighter learning formats such as:

- cybersecurity podcasts
- technical videos
- professional discussions
- industry content
- audio reviews
- career and market observations

These formats do not replace structured study or practical work, but they help maintain contact with the field while reducing cognitive overload.

## From Quantity to Quality

My approach has gradually shifted from:

More hours  
More notes  
More topics  
More pressure

toward:

Better priorities  
More practical connection  
Structured review  
Better recovery  
More sustainable progress

The objective is no longer to understand everything immediately.

The objective is to build strong foundations, identify gaps, return to important topics when necessary, and continue developing consistently.

## Current Approach

Today, I organize my learning around daily and weekly priorities rather than trying to progress in every area simultaneously.

Some topics are active, some are paused, and others are intentionally left for later.

This allows me to maintain direction without treating every learning objective as urgent.

My cybersecurity transition continues to evolve, and I expect the methodology to continue changing as my technical knowledge and practical experience grow.

The most important principle I have learned so far is simple:

**One step at a time.**
