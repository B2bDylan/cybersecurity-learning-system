# Learning Workflow

This document describes the learning workflow I have developed during my transition into cybersecurity.

It was not designed from the beginning as a fixed system. It evolved gradually as I learned how to balance theory, hands-on practice, documentation, review, language development, and limited study time.

## Starting Point

One of my first challenges was understanding where to begin.

Cybersecurity includes many different domains, learning paths, certifications, tools, and specializations. For someone entering the field from another professional background, choosing a structured starting point can be difficult.

I began with the Google Cybersecurity Professional Certificate because it provided a broad introduction to cybersecurity concepts and helped me build an initial framework for understanding the field.

At the same time, I recognized that theoretical knowledge alone would not be enough.

This led me to combine structured study with practical learning through platforms such as TryHackMe.

## Theory and Practice

During the first stage of my learning journey, my study time was approximately divided between:

- structured cybersecurity study
- hands-on practice
- personal notes and review

Initially, most of my attention was focused on the Google Cybersecurity Certificate, while a smaller portion was dedicated to TryHackMe.

At one point, I temporarily reduced hands-on practice to progress faster through the theoretical material. Later, I realized that this created an imbalance.

As practical labs became more relevant in my studies, I reintroduced TryHackMe and began exploring networking concepts more actively.

I also started using tools such as Wireshark in my own authorized home environment to observe network traffic and connect theoretical networking concepts with real activity.

Seeing a network operate in practice made concepts such as protocols, traffic, devices, and communication patterns much more tangible.

## Building Stronger Foundations

As my studies progressed, I realized how important networking, infrastructure, operating systems, and hardware fundamentals are for understanding cybersecurity.

This changed my learning priorities.

Rather than treating every cybersecurity topic with the same depth immediately, I began to recognize the value of building stronger foundational knowledge first and returning to specialized topics later.

My current objective is to complete my main cybersecurity foundation while progressively strengthening my networking knowledge.

## Documentation Workflow

Another major adjustment was learning how to use technology to support the learning process itself.

My documentation process currently includes:

1. Study the source material
2. Take personal notes
3. Identify the most important concepts
4. Rewrite the material in my own words
5. Use AI-assisted tools to improve structure and review
6. Create TXT or PDF study references when useful
7. Revisit concepts through repetition
8. Practice in authorized environments when possible
9. Remove restricted, private, or assessment-related information
10. Transform appropriate learning outcomes into public-safe documentation

AI-assisted tools are used to support review, organization, repetition, and clarity. They do not replace the original study process or independent learning.

## Multiple Learning Formats

I have found that using different formats improves my ability to revisit and understand technical concepts.

Depending on the topic, I may use:

- written notes
- TXT summaries
- PDF study documents
- AI-assisted reviews
- interactive audio and podcast-style reviews
- practical labs
- diagrams and visual references
- YouTube technical content
- cybersecurity podcasts
- professional discussions on LinkedIn
- public cybersecurity awareness content

Using multiple formats helps reduce repetition fatigue and allows me to approach the same concept from different perspectives.

## Bilingual Learning

Most of my cybersecurity studies are conducted in English, while Portuguese is my native language.

This creates an additional learning layer.

Technical study often involves:

- understanding the concept
- learning the English terminology
- translating the idea accurately
- being able to explain it clearly in Portuguese
- improving my professional English at the same time

Rather than treating this only as a difficulty, I now use it as part of the learning process.

Explaining technical concepts in simpler language helps reinforce whether I actually understand them.

## Learning Through Communication

The Why Not Cyber project became another part of this methodology.

The project focuses on translating cybersecurity concepts into accessible language for people outside the technical field.

Creating awareness content forces me to take a technical subject, identify its essential meaning, and explain it without unnecessary complexity.

This has become another form of active review.

Professional platforms such as LinkedIn also help me observe discussions, learning methods, career paths, technologies, and perspectives from students and cybersecurity professionals.

## Knowledge Organization

I use Start.me as a central dashboard for organizing cybersecurity resources.

The dashboard helps me group references related to areas such as:

- networking
- defensive security
- threats
- governance, risk, and compliance
- frameworks
- standards
- regulations
- learning platforms
- technical references

This reduces the time spent searching for previously discovered resources and gives me a centralized view of my learning environment.

## Physical Learning Environment

My learning system is not completely digital.

I also use a whiteboard next to my workspace to organize:

- weekly priorities
- current topics
- paused topics
- pending activities
- short reminders
- study goals

I also keep selected technical references visible around my workspace, such as networking models and protocol concepts.

These act as quick visual reminders and make important concepts easier to revisit during normal study sessions.

## Continuous Improvement

The methodology continues to evolve.

The objective is not to study as much information as possible.

The objective is to build understanding gradually, connect theory with practice, document meaningful progress, and develop a sustainable learning system over time.

One step at a time.
